# picchain — Improvement Backlog

A running list of improvements to the chain, each traceable to a measured
shortfall. Ordered by expected value. An entry leaves this list by being
implemented and tested, or by being rejected with the reason recorded.

Entries are sanitised: they describe the toolchain, and project specifics stay
in the project tree.

## 1. Adopt a GPU FDTD backend (Tidy3D adapter)

The `fdtd` stage accounts for 23 of 38 hours of stage compute across two
recent design campaigns, from 20 runs, at 69 minutes each. Two solves of a
coupling-constant measurement failed on cost alone: an 18-hour band-structure
run at coarse resolution, and a reflectance run stopped at 6 hours against an
estimated 35. The measurement had to be transferred from a neighbouring
geometry on a stated assumption. A cloud GPU solver removes the chain's
weakest axis and is complementary to it, the chain supplying the verification
layer the solver ecosystems do not carry.

## 2. Cache stage results keyed on resolved inputs

A full factorial corner sweep re-runs every requested stage at every corner.
The mode stage ran 135 times in one campaign, mostly on inputs identical to a
previous run: a corner that perturbs only the electrode gap cannot change the
mode solve. Key each stage's result on a hash of the inputs it actually reads
and the factorial cost collapses toward the number of distinct cross-sections.
This converts the process window from a once-per-release exercise into a
per-iteration check, and the factorial is the chain's most differentiating
feature.

## 3. Model longitudinal gradients, not only levels

Every process parameter is a scalar: a cross-section times a length. A corner
sweep therefore moves the level of the film thickness and can never represent
a thickness gradient along a device, which for a long grating is the dominant
failure (the Bragg phase budget measured on a recent mirror was 0.117 nm of
film against a process specified in nanometres). Present state: the `mode`
stage solves dn_eff/d(film) on request, the `grating` stage converts it to a
uniformity budget and compares a declared non-uniformity. Wanted: a transfer-
matrix grating solve over a declared thickness profile, so the degraded
reflectivity and stop band are computed rather than bounded.

## 4. Radiation loss of high-order gratings

Coupled-mode theory keeps the backward Bragg order; every lower diffraction
order of an order-m grating radiates, and no stage computes it. Present
state: the `grating` stage names the unmodelled orders and points at the
threshold-gain margin as the budget. Wanted: a 2D solve of the radiated
fraction per unit length, even approximate, so the budget is compared against
an estimate rather than standing alone. Depends usefully on item 1.

## 5. Yield, not only corners (Monte Carlo over targets)

The corner sweep answers "does every corner pass". A fabrication decision
wants "what fraction of die meet spec". Sampling the declared process
distributions through the chain, with the severity-tagged targets evaluated
per sample, gives yield per requirement - a composition of the existing
`corners` and `verify` machinery. Practical only after item 2 (or on
surrogates, item 6).

## 6. Calibrated closed-form surrogates for sweep tiers

Corner and Monte Carlo tiers do not need the full solver at every point.
Calibrate the closed forms against a handful of full solves at the nominal
and the extremes, sweep on the surrogate, and re-verify the worst points with
the full solver. Report the surrogate's calibration error alongside, in the
same spirit as the existing convergence guards.

## 7. Scaffold command and a minimal design file

A released design file approaches two thousand lines, most of it earned
commentary. A newcomer cannot start from that. `picchain new <name>` should
emit a minimal viable design (tens of lines) that runs end to end, and the
commentary should accrue with decisions rather than arrive up front.

## 8. One reviewer-facing artefact

The design report is hundreds of lines of Markdown and the dashboard is per-
design HTML. What gets forwarded to a decision-maker is neither. A single
self-contained page per release - verdict, corner window, deck and layer
coverage, monitor inventory, the run-of-record checksum - generated by the
`release` stage as part of the manifest.

## 9. Cross-check the layer map against an installed PDK

The layer map is hand-typed and was twice wrong in ways only measurement
caught. Where a machine-readable PDK for the declared stack is installed,
validate `layout.layer_map` against it at load time, in the same spirit as
the platform-to-deck bind.

## 10. Optical-access check for anything that guides light

A mask carried four optical monitor structures with no optical port; the
defect was found by an external reviewer, twice (first on the device ladder,
then on the monitor field). Wanted: a layout-stage check that every structure
tagged as optically measured has a guide reaching a declared facet line, so
"drawable but unreadable" becomes a reported condition rather than a
discovery on returned silicon.

## 11. Generate the compliance table, never transcribe it

Two design reports carried a hand-maintained requirements table against a
`metrics.verify.rows` that had moved on twice. The tables quoted a tuning
coefficient 17 % below what the design delivered and an excursion 30 % above,
and both documents had been reread several times without the drift being seen.
Rereading does not find this class of defect, because the table is internally
consistent and only its relationship to the run is wrong.

`tools/corner_heatmap.py` and a per-project `compliance_table.py` were written
to close it, and the report now carries the command that regenerates the table
beside the table. **The general form belongs in the chain**: a `picchain table`
command emitting the acceptance rows of a named run as Markdown, so a document
holds a generated block rather than a transcription. The same argument applies
to any figure a report quotes from a run.

## 12. Warn where two metrics are computed from the same expression

A metric added to describe a new architecture was computed from the quantity it
was meant to replace, and it therefore returned a number identical to the row
beside it, to every digit. Two rows agreeing exactly is either a duplicate or a
coincidence, and it was read as corroboration for a full design cycle.

A cheap guard exists: at the end of a stage, compare the emitted scalars
pairwise and warn where two distinct names carry bit-identical values. Distinct
quantities of a real design almost never agree to the last bit, so the false
positive rate is low and the failure it catches is expensive.

## 13. Draw the process window, do not tabulate it

A factorial over four parameters at three levels lays out as a 9 x 9 grid, so
every corner keeps its own cell and a failure keeps its address. Rendered that
way, a corner sweep that had been reported for weeks as "six failures" showed
at a glance that the failing metric does not depend on one of the four
parameters at all: the map is striped, the rows repeat in groups of three, and
the remedy is therefore process control rather than geometry. No table of
minima and maxima carries that.

`tools/corner_heatmap.py` implements it and takes any factorial sweep over four
parameters. It belongs behind `picchain corners --plot`.

## 14. The chain should own the figure copy, not the operator

A design publishes its figures from `<design>/figures/`, and that directory is a
hand-made copy of `runs/<id>/figures/`. Nothing keeps the two in step, so the
copy silently ages: two designs were re-run to a new operating point and their
documents kept displaying pictures of the device as it had been three days
earlier, including a cross-section at an electrode gap that had since changed.

`tools/check_figures_current.py` closes it by comparison and `--sync`, and it
belongs inside the chain. **`picchain report` already regenerates the figures; it
should also publish them**, and `picchain release` should refuse a design whose
published figures differ from the run being released. A release is a claim about
a set of artifacts, and the pictures are artifacts.

The tool's second output is the more interesting one: the figures **no stage
produces**. Those are refreshed by nothing and are where a hand-made screenshot
hides. Two such figures were found showing a device ladder that had been reset
the same day, and nothing in the repository could regenerate them.

## 15. Store the arrays a figure needs, rather than reconstructing them

A tuning curve drawn from a stage's reported slope is a restatement of a number.
The same curve drawn from the stage's stored mode track is a measurement, and it
shows what a scalar cannot: where the mode hop falls, how much of the drive lies
either side of it, and whether a second track runs unbroken through it.

The cavity stage was extended to store the mode track taken with an intracavity
phase section driven, because the figure that matters compares it against the
track without. **The general rule is that a stage stores what a figure of it
would need**, and the test is whether a plotting tool can be written that reads
only arrays and never re-derives physics. A plotting tool that recomputes is a
second implementation of the model, and it will diverge.

## 16. Stream an external solver's output instead of buffering it to the end

`fdtd/bridge.py` invokes meep through `subprocess.run(capture_output=True)`, so
the solver's step output is held in memory and written to `meep_<name>_run.log`
only when the process exits. **A solve that runs for an hour is silent for that
hour**, and meep prints exactly the progress an operator needs: the timestep
reached, the elapsed simulation time, and the field decay the stopping condition
is waiting on.

The cost of that silence was measured. A taper solve was checked against the host
process list, which showed nothing because eight ranks were in a synchronisation
barrier, and it was nearly reported as having died; the remaining time then had to
be estimated from the cell size, the Courant timestep and the enforced minimum run
time rather than read from the solver.

Replace the call with a `Popen` that tees each line to the log as it arrives. The
result file and the timeout behave as before, and a running solve becomes
observable by `tail`. This also makes the timeout diagnosable: a job killed at the
ceiling currently leaves a log written from a buffer that was never flushed, so
the one case where the output matters most is the case where it is least
complete.

## 17. Give warnings identity, then make silence fail

`ctx.warn(msg: str)` appends free prose to a list, from ninety call sites across
seventeen stages, and until 2026-08-17 no code read that list. The acceptance
verdict grades targets; a finding carrying no threshold has no owner at all.

**Phase one, done.** `tools/warning_inventory.py` takes the inventory and
attributes each warning to its stage by parsing the literals out of every
`ctx.warn` site, so runs already made are covered without re-running. Two
designs returned 33 findings, 0 acknowledged.

**Phase two.** Give each site a stable key, `ctx.warn(key, msg)`, and let a
design acknowledge findings by key with the reason each is accepted. `verify`
then fails on any emitted key the design has not acknowledged. The value is not
in recording the known findings; it is that **a newly appearing warning breaks
the run**, which is the only mechanism that catches what nobody anticipated.

Keys also make findings diffable between runs and between designs, which is how
the two genuine defects of 2026-08-17 were separated from the thirty-one
properties of the architecture: seven keys were common to both devices, and the
two that were not turned out to be the bugs.

## 18. Extend preflight from geometry to provenance and units

`picchain.preflight` refuses a design whose declared fields contradict each
other, before any solver starts. Seven checks cover geometric fit and declared
ranges. Three further classes belong there and are not yet written.

* **Provenance.** A design that forbids unconfirmed materials while naming one
  is a contradiction knowable from the file, and it currently surfaces only after
  the chain has run.
* **Units and magnitude.** A length declared in millimetres where micrometres are
  meant passes every type check and fails no bound. A plausibility range per
  field would catch the class outright.
* **Deck and layer agreement.** The layer map is already bound to the platform;
  the same comparison against the rule deck's own layer numbers belongs before
  the mask is drawn rather than after the deck runs.

The discipline that generates these: **audit the silent repairs.** Every `max(`,
`min(` or default applied to a value that came from the design file marks a place
where an impossible input was anticipated and tolerated. Two of the three found
on this pass were promoted to preconditions.
