# picchain — PIC Design Chain

A fully scriptable design, simulation and verification chain for photonic
integrated circuits. It is assembled from the open toolset catalogued in
[joamatab/awesome_photonics](https://github.com/joamatab/awesome_photonics).

Execution proceeds without a graphical interface and without a licence server.
One YAML file is supplied as input, one JSON metric tree is returned as output,
and a non-zero exit code is returned where a design fails its acceptance targets.

> This document is the reference manual and is what an operator needs. A
> separate operating manual for autonomous agents is held outside this
> repository. The applicable
> document. It, rather than this file, constitutes the operating manual.

## Scope in One Sentence

One class of device is taken end to end at present, being a hybrid extended-DBR
Pockels laser on a thin-film electro-optic platform, from the waveguide
cross-section through to an assembled die with its frame, its process control
monitors and a submission manifest. Everything else on a photonic circuit is
either absent or represented by a declared assumption.

## What Is Computed

Seventeen stages are executed in dependency order, of which nine are disabled
by default. The model employed by each is stated below, together with the
approximation that limits it, so that a figure is not read as carrying more
authority than the model behind it supports.

| stage | what is computed | model employed | principal approximation | implemented in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mode` | n_eff and n_g of the ridge, the index perturbation Δn_eff contributed by the Bragg posts, confinement factors, guided-mode count, and the field on the mesh | semi-vectorial finite-difference mode solver in the Stern formulation, with a diagonal anisotropic permittivity, on a graded tensor-product mesh with sub-pixel material averaging | two dimensions only. The cross-section is assumed invariant along the propagation direction. Polarisation hybridisation is not represented. Metal is treated as a real permittivity of unity, the electrodes being distant from the ridge | [`solvers/fdmode.py`](src/picchain/solvers/fdmode.py), [`geometry.py`](src/picchain/geometry.py) |
| `taper` | conversion between guided modes along the input taper, the local adiabaticity margin against the slab, and the width at which that margin is worst | eigenmode expansion over the local modes of a staircase of slices, all rasterised onto one mesh, with the propagation phase retained and reflection neglected | a guided-mode basis. Radiation into the continuum is outside it, so the computed loss is a lower bound and the adiabaticity margin is the figure that indicates where radiation is to be expected. Disabled by default, costing one mode solve per slice | [`eme.py`](src/picchain/eme.py) |
| `fem` | the same cross-section re-solved by an independent method: the effective index, the index perturbation Δn_eff contributed by the posts, the confinement, the polarisation purity, a mesh-convergence guard and the anisotropy bracket, each set against the finite-difference figure | full-vectorial finite elements by <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>femwell</strong></span> and <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>scikit-fem</strong></span>, on an unstructured triangulation conforming to the polygon edges, generated by <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>gmsh</strong></span> | one scalar permittivity per element, so the anisotropic problem cannot be posed. The solve is performed at the principal axis the semi-vectorial operator reads, which makes the comparison like-for-like, and the second axis is solved separately to bracket what the scalar model omits. Disabled by default. No downstream stage reads its output | **femwell** (annex), wrapped by [`solvers/femmode.py`](src/picchain/solvers/femmode.py) |
| `fdtd` | with `structure: taper`, the transmission of the taper into the fundamental mode, the total flux, the reflection, and the difference between the two, which is the radiated power. With `structure: grating`, the reflection spectrum of a finite grating and the κ it implies, set against the coupled-mode value computed on the identical two-dimensional structure. With `structure: bandstructure`, κ from the photonic band gap of one period by <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>MPB</strong></span>, which carries neither a length nor a radiation channel | finite-difference time domain, by <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>meep</strong></span>, executed in its own environment. Two dimensions by effective index, the two slab indices being solved from the same cross-section the rest of the chain uses; three dimensions builds the layer stack instead | in two dimensions only the lateral radiation channel is present. The solver is external, so the stage reports a stated reason rather than a traceback where it cannot be reached. Disabled by default: minutes in two dimensions, and an offline job in three. The band gap is the difference of two nearly degenerate bands and converges slowly, so the structure is solved a second time on a coarser mesh; `fdtd.convergence.resolved` states whether the comparison distinguishes anything, and an unresolved comparison is reported as unresolved | **meep** and **MPB** (annex), driven by [`fdtd/`](src/picchain/fdtd/) |
| `bend` | the bend mode at each declared radius, its outward shift, the change in effective index, and the position of the radiation caustic | conformal transformation of the bend into an equivalent straight guide, graded as exp(x/R), solved by the same mode solver | a bound-mode method. Where the caustic falls inside the window the mode is leaky and the transformation manufactures a state bound to the wall; the solver refuses that regime with a stated reason rather than answering it. No loss in decibels per turn is produced. Disabled by default | [`solvers/fdmode.py`](src/picchain/solvers/fdmode.py) |
| `facet` | the coupling across the facet, separated into mode overlap, Fresnel reflection and the angled-facet penalty, with the alignment the assembly must hold | the guide mode is solved at the taper tip, where the facet actually is; the partner is described by its mode-field diameters and approximated by an elliptical Gaussian. Snell gives the refraction, and across a declared gap the beam arrives displaced, so `facet.offset_x_um` states where the partner is placed and the overlap sees the difference | the partner mode is declared rather than solved, and the approximation is recorded in the metrics. Disabled by default, since a figure produced from an unstated assumption is worse than none | [`coupling.py`](src/picchain/coupling.py) |
| `grating` | period, coupling constant κ, κL, Bragg wavelength, peak reflectivity, bandwidth, sidelobe suppression, penetration depth, group delay, transform-limit floor | coupled-mode theory, with κ taken from the closed-form Fourier coefficient of the longitudinal index profile at the working order, and a piecewise transfer-matrix evaluation supporting apodisation, chirp and loss | the grating is treated as a perturbation of the guided mode. Radiation into the cladding and out-of-plane scatter at the posts are excluded. **The longitudinal profile is assumed rectangular.** The suppression a rounded profile imposes carries the order squared, so an assumption that costs the first harmonic 8 % costs the third 54 %. `grating.profile_sigma_um` declares a smoothing length and defaults to zero, which is the rectangular case | [`tmm.py`](src/picchain/tmm.py) |
| `eo` | electro-optic overlap Γ, tuning in MHz/V, Vπ·L with and without the overlap derating, capacitance, a lumped RC bandwidth, and the travelling-wave quantities: microwave index, characteristic impedance, conductor loss and the electro-optic 3 dB bandwidth | anisotropic finite-volume electrostatics on a coarse RF mesh, interpolated onto the fine optical mesh for the overlap. The inductance comes from the same geometry solved with the dielectrics removed, since a magnetostatic problem does not see them | quasi-static, so the line parameters are those of a transverse-electromagnetic mode and no substrate mode or radiation is represented. The fringing field is truncated by the window, which under-reads the capacitance | [`solvers/electrostatic.py`](src/picchain/solvers/electrostatic.py), [`rf.py`](src/picchain/rf.py) |
| `circuit` | the passive circuit assembled from scattering matrices: the reflection presented to the gain chip, the ripple the residual facet reflection imposes on it, and the residual against the closed-form two-mirror result | a netlist solved by <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>SAX</strong></span>, with the grating taken from the transfer-matrix spectrum already computed rather than re-modelled | the gain chip is not a scattering block, so gain, saturation and carrier dynamics are outside it and the laser condition is still evaluated by the `cavity` stage. Disabled by default | **SAX** (annex), assembled by [`stages/s16_circuit.py`](src/picchain/stages/s16_circuit.py) |
| `cavity` | free spectral range, Pockels lever, laser tuning rate, the mode-hop-free range measured between mode hops with the zero-bias figure beside it, chirp nonlinearity, threshold gain, Schawlow-Townes-Henry linewidth, SMSR | round-trip phase of a two-element cavity, solved numerically against the complex mirror response across a voltage sweep, with the longitudinal modes tracked individually | steady state and single mode. No rate equations, no relative intensity noise, no self-injection locking, no thermal drift. The gain chip is represented by scalar parameters that are design inputs rather than computed quantities | [`stages/s04_cavity.py`](src/picchain/stages/s04_cavity.py) |
| `dynamics` | the photon lifetime of the composite cavity, the threshold current, the light-current curve and slope efficiency, the relaxation oscillation with its damping and the small-signal bandwidth that follows, the relative intensity noise spectrum, and the optical-feedback parameter with the regime it places the laser in | the single-mode rate equations for carrier and photon density, solved in closed form at the steady state, with the photon lifetime taken from the round-trip survival so that the passive feed, the coupling losses and the DBR are already inside it, and the gain scaled by the active fraction of the round trip | single mode and steady state. No differential equation is integrated, so mode partition and the transient during a chirp ramp are outside it. The gain-chip parameters are properties of a part rather than of the photonic design, and are assumptions until a datasheet replaces them. Disabled by default | [`laser.py`](src/picchain/laser.py) |
| `layout` | the mask polygons for the device, emitted as GDSII and OASIS with the layer table beside them; the drawn, printed and nominal geometries side by side; the angled facets and the crystal orientation key; whether every grating period was drawn; the exclusive-or between the two backends; the manufacturing-grid snap and its cost; and the geometry a rule deck does not examine | explicit polygon construction from the design parameters, in the **drawn** frame, which differs from the nominal one wherever a process bias is declared and pre-compensation is requested. Each layer is merged and then snapped, a boolean introducing vertices at intersections that the drawing never placed | a fixed E-DBR floor plan is drawn. Arbitrary circuit topology is not assembled here. `layout.draw_periods` draws the whole device by default, emitting all 5,665 periods costing about 1.5 s; `layout.require_complete` refuses a partial mask, and any shortfall is reported on every run either way | **gdsfactory** and **KLayout** (annex), driven by [`stages/s05_layout.py`](src/picchain/stages/s05_layout.py) and [`process.py`](src/picchain/process.py) |
| `reticle` | the die: the device cell placed, a ladder of device copies stepped across a declared parameter, a seal ring, a dicing lane, nested overlay marks at four corners, a die label rendered as polygons, and four families of process control monitor | explicit polygon construction, with the device imported as a cell instance. Each monitor is held to the rule deck declared for the device, and any dimension raised to meet it is reported | fill is sized by the `mask` stage and not placed here. No step and repeat across a reticle field is performed. Disabled by default, a die floor plan being a decision rather than a default | [`monitors.py`](src/picchain/monitors.py) |
| `drc` | per-rule violation counts, with a GDS marker layer for inspection, evaluated against the device cell or against the assembled die | the <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>klayout</strong></span> Region engine, against rules declared in the design file | the rules are those declared by the operator, no foundry rule deck being bundled. A clean result attests only to the rules supplied and to the periods actually drawn | **KLayout** (annex), rules by [`stages/s06_drc.py`](src/picchain/stages/s06_drc.py) |
| `mask` | connected regions per layer, an extracted netlist in which declared layer pairs are joined where they overlap, overlaps between layers that must not touch, and the density of each layer tile by tile with the fill area any deficit requires | <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>KLayout</strong></span> Region operations and the <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>KLayout</strong></span> netlist extractor on the emitted GDS | fill is sized, not placed. Density is evaluated on a fixed tile grid rather than the sliding window some foundries specify. The extraction carries no device recognition, so it establishes connectivity and not a comparison against a schematic. The expected counts must be stated correctly, which is itself a statement of what the device is | **KLayout** (annex), driven by [`stages/s12_mask.py`](src/picchain/stages/s12_mask.py) |
| `verify` | the per-target table and the run verdict | evaluation of each declared acceptance target against the metric tree | the verdict tests what was declared. A failure mode for which no target was written is not detected | [`stages/s07_verify.py`](src/picchain/stages/s07_verify.py) |
| `release` | the submission manifest: every artifact with its SHA-256, the design and environment that produced it, and ten conditions of readiness | inventory and hashing, with each condition read from the metric tree | it asserts that a set of files is ready to be sent, and no more. A waived condition is recorded as waived. Disabled by default | [`stages/s15_release.py`](src/picchain/stages/s15_release.py) |

The chain executes in approximately 50 s on a single core for the nine stages
enabled by default. Each optional stage is switched on per design: `taper` and
`bend` cost one mode solve per slice or radius, `fem` four finite-element
solves, `fdtd` an external solver, and `circuit` and `release` seconds.

One hundred and thirty-six tests are provided. The solvers are held to analytic
results: the two mode solvers against the slab dispersion relation, the
electrostatics against a parallel-plate capacitor, the transfer matrix against
`tanh²(κL)`, the eigenmode expansion against the Fresnel coefficients, and the
circuit assembly against the closed-form two-mirror result. The mask work is
held to the geometric property each structure is required to have, measured off
the written file rather than off the parameters that produced it.
End-to-end validation against a published and measured device is recorded in
[`../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/TOOLCHAIN_VALIDATION.md`](../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/TOOLCHAIN_VALIDATION.md).

## What Is Not Computed

The following capabilities are absent. Each is stated with the design decision it
consequently cannot support, so that the gap is visible before a number is
trusted rather than afterwards.

| absent capability | consequence | what is affected | route to closing it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Device recognition.** Connectivity is extracted, named from text labels and compared against a declared schematic. What is not done is recognising a device: no transistor, diode or resistor is identified | a short through the wrong component presents as a correct net. The comparison establishes connectivity and not function | any circuit carrying active devices | <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>KLayout</strong></span> device extractors, the netlist machinery being already in use |
| **Thermal and thermo-optic behaviour** | self-heating, drift and crosstalk between adjacent devices are not predicted | thermal tuning, the flatness of a sweep against drive frequency, and long-term stability | a heat-equation stage on the existing mesh, or `femwell` |
| **A geometry description for components other than a width taper.** The propagation solver is present and is exercised by `fdtd`; what is missing is the description of the other structures | multimode interference couplers, bends, directional couplers and facet coupling are not simulated. The loss figures carried for them in a design file are declared assumptions annotated `# ASSUMPTION:`, not computed results | any insertion loss budget, any splitter ratio, and any coupling efficiency to a fibre or to a gain chip | further geometry builders for the same runner, the solver and the bridge being in place. `tidy3d` was surveyed and set aside, its solver being a paid cloud service to which the geometry would have to be uploaded |
| **The vertical radiation channel of a taper, by default** | `fdtd` runs in two dimensions by effective index unless `fdtd.dimensions: 3` is set, so radiation out of the plane is not counted | any absolute taper loss where the vertical confinement is weak | the three-dimensional path, which is implemented and is a matter of run time rather than of capability |
| **A three-dimensional microwave solve** | the transmission-line model is present and gives the microwave index, impedance, conductor loss and bandwidth. What it does not carry is the transverse field pattern at microwave frequencies, the substrate mode, or radiation from the line | electrode geometries far from the quasi-static regime, and any structure where the line couples to something other than itself | a microwave eigenmode solve, for which **palace** or **elmer** was surveyed |
| **Thermal, stress and piezoelectric models** | self-heating, thermo-optic drift and acoustic resonances are not predicted. The bulk-acoustic resonance at 19.8 MHz and the mechanical mode at 70 kHz measured in the reference paper do not appear in any metric | thermal tuning, the flatness of a frequency chirp against drive frequency, long-term wavelength stability, and any thermal crosstalk between adjacent devices | a coupled thermal and mechanical FEM stage, for which `femwell` is already declared as an extra |
| **Full-vector and magneto-optic modelling** | the mode solver is semi-vectorial with a diagonal permittivity tensor, which is correct for quasi-TE on X-cut lithium niobate or tantalate | strongly hybridised modes, polarisation rotation, and any non-reciprocal device such as a Ce:YIG isolator, which requires off-diagonal tensor elements | a full-vector solver admitting a non-diagonal permittivity |
| **Laser dynamics** | the cavity model is steady-state and single-mode. Turn-on transients, relaxation oscillation, relative intensity noise, mode-partition behaviour and self-injection locking are excluded. The SMSR figure scales with the assumed spontaneous emission factor | any dynamic specification. The SMSR is to be read as an ordering between designs rather than as an absolute value | a rate-equation stage, driven by the same cavity parameters |
| **Circuit-level assembly** | one device is modelled at a time. Several components are not connected into a system and evaluated together | a full transceiver, a resonator coupled to a bus, an interferometer, or any layout containing more than the single E-DBR floor plan | `sax` for scattering-matrix assembly. It is declared as an extra and is not yet wired to a stage |
| **A foundry process design kit** | the material database carries a `provenance` field, and entries not traceable to a source are tagged `needs_confirmation` and reported on every run | absolute accuracy of every index-derived quantity. Differences evaluated on a shared mesh are considerably more reliable than absolute values | replacement of the tagged entries with foundry process control monitor data |
| **Statistical tolerance analysis** | a one-parameter sweep is available and is the mechanism by which sensitivity is quantified. A Monte Carlo over a correlated set of process variations is not provided | yield estimation, and any statement of the form "90 % of die will meet this specification" | repeated invocation of the sweep facility, or a dedicated stage |

The frame and the process control structures that stood at the head of this
list are now drawn by the `reticle` stage: a seal ring, a dicing lane, nested
overlay marks, a die label, and monitors for the coupling constant against gap,
the propagation loss by cut-back, the printed critical dimension against the
drawn one, and the electro-optic overlap against electrode gap.

Fill placement, mask polarity by derived layer, connectivity against a declared
schematic, the submission manifest and circuit assembly by <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>SAX</strong></span> have all been
supplied. What the fill placer still needs is a foundry pattern rather than the
placeholder it defaults to.

Both of the low-cost cross-checks that stood ahead of these gaps have now been
taken. An independent evaluation of κ by Bloch band structure is provided by the
`fdtd` stage under `structure: bandstructure`, and an independent evaluation of
the cross-section by finite elements is provided by the `fem` stage. The
travelling-wave electrode model has also been supplied. What remains, in order,
is circuit-level assembly, then a thermal and mechanical stage. The survey of the
catalogue from which this ordering was drawn is held in
[`../docs/design_simulation_verification_flow.md` §2](../docs/design_simulation_verification_flow.md).

### Limits of what is implemented

The table above lists capabilities that are absent. The following are properties
of what is present, and each bounds a conclusion that the presence of the feature
might otherwise be taken to support. **This list and the table above are the
single source for the limits of this chain**; the operating manual states the
obligations that follow from them and does not restate them.

* **The mode solver is semi-vectorial with a diagonal permittivity tensor.**
  This is correct for quasi-TE on X-cut lithium niobate or tantalate. The `fem`
  stage measures the polarisation purity, which is the quantity bounding the
  error of that assumption on any given cross-section, and it is to be read
  before the assumption is relied upon.
* **The finite-element cross-check carries one scalar permittivity per element**,
  so it cannot pose the anisotropic problem the finite-difference solver poses.
  It is run at the principal axis the semi-vectorial operator reads, which makes
  the comparison like-for-like, and the second principal axis is solved
  separately to bracket the anisotropic answer. The bracket weighted by the
  polarisation purity is `fem.anisotropy_bracket`.
* **The lumped electrode estimate is not a bandwidth.** `eo.lumped_RC_bandwidth_MHz`
  truncates the fringing field at the electrostatic window and is an
  over-estimate at the lower end. Above a few millimetres of electrode the
  structure is a transmission line and the travelling-wave figure is the one to
  read; the two are reported side by side and a warning is raised where they
  diverge.
* **The rate equations are single-mode and are solved in closed form at a steady
  state.** Mode competition, mode partition and the transient during a chirp ramp
  are outside them, and no ordinary differential equation is integrated. The
  feedback classification is a criterion applied to the computed C parameter and
  to the reflector ratio. It is not a stability analysis of the Lang-Kobayashi
  equations. A relaxation oscillation falling near the compound mode spacing is
  warned about rather than modelled, that being the condition under which the
  single-mode assumption fails.
* **The die orientation with respect to the crystal is drawn and reported, and it
  is not modelled.** The permittivity tensor is not rotated, so a misaligned
  device is flagged through `layout.crystal_misalignment_deg` rather than
  computed.
* **Fill is sized and not placed.** The area each deficient tile requires is
  reported. Placement requires a foundry pattern and its exclusions around a
  guide and around a pad.
* **No reticle-level step and repeat is performed.** One die is assembled.
  Arraying it across a reticle field is done against the foundry frame.
* **The monitors measure four quantities and no others**: coupling constant
  against gap, propagation loss by cut-back, printed critical dimension against
  drawn, and electro-optic overlap against electrode gap. Where a monitor cannot
  be drawn at the dimension requested, the dimension is raised to the declared
  rule and the compromise is reported under `gaps_raised_to_the_rule`. A monitor
  is drawn against the rules the design declares, so where those are looser than
  the process rules the monitor itself may be unmanufacturable.

### Limits that attach to a foundry rule deck

* **The layer table defaults to placeholder numbers.** A deck reads layers by
  number, so placeholders do not merely fail to match, they collide: a deck will
  read whichever of its own layers those numbers correspond to, report violations
  on the wrong geometry, and examine none of the right geometry. **That is a
  false pass and is worse than running no deck.** A design going to a process
  replaces the numbers with that process's own. Mask polarity remains
  unexpressed: every layer is drawn positive, so a process wanting a dark field
  or a trench layer needs the map replaced as well.
* **A runset written for the graphical application names no input and no report
  file.** It expects a layout already open and a marker browser. The chain
  supplies `source($input)` and a report file, alters nothing else, and records
  what it added in `drc.deck_io_bound`.
* **A runset checks a chip and not a device cell.** Footprint, centring and
  exclusion-ring rules are evaluated against `CHIP_OUTER` and `CHIP_INNER`, which
  the `reticle` stage draws only where `reticle.chip_frame.enabled` is set. A
  deck run against a device cell reports nothing on those rules, and **that
  silence is not a pass**.

## Reading a Result with the Correct Confidence

Six mechanisms are provided by which the standing of a number can be
established, and each is to be consulted before a figure is used.

1. **The severity of the target it was tested against.** A verdict of PASS
   asserts only that every target of severity `must` was met. Targets of severity
   `should` are reported and counted, and the verdict is not affected by them.
2. **The provenance of the materials it depends on.** Entries tagged
   `needs_confirmation` are listed on every run, and a hard block may be imposed
   by setting `allow_unconfirmed_materials: false` in the design.
3. **The assumption annotations in the design file.** Any quantity not traceable
   to the literature, to a statement of requirements or to a foundry document
   carries an `# ASSUMPTION:` comment. The linewidth and the SMSR in particular
   are computed from gain-chip parameters that are assumptions.
4. **The warnings.** Failure modes that do not present as a failed target are
   emitted to `metrics.warnings`. The set includes a divergence in the guided
   mode count, a computed bandwidth below the transform limit, an unapodised
   grating whose sidelobes will appear as chirp nonlinearity, optical absorption
   by the electrode metal, and an electrode too slow for the chirp harmonics.
5. **Whether the mask examined is the mask that would be sent.**
   `layout.mask_is_complete` states whether every grating period was drawn;
   `layout.backend_xor.agree` whether the two writers produced the same
   geometry; `drc.checked` and `mask.checked` whether the device cell or the
   assembled die carried the verdict; `layout.process.identity` whether the
   drawn and printed geometries coincide; `layout.grid` what snapping to the
   manufacturing grid cost and how far it dithered the period;
   `layout.geometry.clean` whether the shapes are shapes. Seven figures that
   are ordinarily assumed are thereby reported instead.
6. **Whether a second instrument has been applied to it.** Two quantities can be
   computed twice by unrelated methods. The cross-section is solved by finite
   differences and, under the `fem` stage, by finite elements. The coupling
   constant is constructed by coupled-mode theory and, under the `fdtd` stage,
   measured from the photonic band gap. A figure that has survived both is of a
   different standing from one that has not, and a figure whose two instruments
   disagree is of a different standing again. Neither cross-check is read until
   its own convergence guard reports `resolved`.

## Installation

```bash
cd design-chain
python -m venv .venv
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pip install -e .          # Windows
# ./.venv/bin/python -m pip install -e .                # Linux/macOS
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pip install -e ".[layout]"   # with gdsfactory
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pip install -e ".[fem]"      # with the FEM cross-check
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli doctor
```

Required: `numpy`, `scipy`, `matplotlib`, `pyyaml`, `pydantic`, `typer`,
`klayout`. Optional: `gdsfactory` (secondary layout backend), `femwell`,
`scikit-fem` and `gmsh` (the `fem` cross-check stage), `sax` (circuit assembly,
not presently wired to a stage).

The `fem` extra installs a mesh generator and a finite-element library, which
together are a larger dependency than the rest of the chain combined. The chain
runs without them and reports their absence through `doctor`; only the `fem`
stage requires them, and it is disabled by default.

### The FDTD Environment

The `fdtd` stage calls <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>meep</strong></span>, which has no Windows build and is therefore held in
a separate environment reached through WSL 2. It is not a dependency of the
chain: where it is absent, the stage reports why and every other stage is
unaffected.

```bash
# inside the WSL distribution, no elevation required
curl -sSL -o ~/.local/bin/micromamba \
    https://github.com/mamba-org/micromamba-releases/releases/latest/download/micromamba-linux-64
chmod +x ~/.local/bin/micromamba
~/.local/bin/micromamba create -y -n mp -c conda-forge \
    "pymeep=*=mpi_mpich_*" python=3.12 numpy scipy h5py
```

Confirm that the chain can reach it:

```bash
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli doctor      # reports the meep version under "fdtd"
```

Behind a TLS-inspecting proxy the distribution will not trust the certificate
chain that Windows already trusts. The organisation's anchors are exported from
the Windows store with `certutil -store -enterprise Root`, concatenated onto
`/etc/ssl/certs/ca-certificates.crt` into a user-owned bundle, and named by
`SSL_CERT_FILE` and by `--ssl-verify`. Verification is not to be disabled.

The distribution, environment name and process count are design fields
(`fdtd.wsl_distro`, `fdtd.environment`, `fdtd.processes`), so a machine that
holds <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>meep</strong></span> natively needs no wrapper at all.

## Execution

```bash
# the design as simulated: the complete device, checked
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli run ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/design.yaml

# the design as it would be submitted: every period, the die frame and its
# monitors, the fill placed, the die checked, and a manifest of what results
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli run ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/design.yaml     --set layout.draw_periods=null --set layout.require_complete=true     --set reticle.enabled=true --set reticle.split.enabled=true     --set drc.target=die --set mask.target=die     --set mask.fill.enabled=true --set release.enabled=true
```

The two differ in what they are for. The first answers whether the design is
right, and emits the device alone. The second answers whether the mask can be
sent, adding the frame, the monitors and the fill, and refusing where it
cannot.

The following are produced beneath
`../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/runs/<timestamp>/`:

```
metrics.json              the complete metric tree, environment fingerprint and warnings
design.resolved.json      the exact inputs, after the `extends:` merge and any --set
```

## Verification Beyond the Nominal Design

Four facilities address the questions a fabrication run asks, as distinct from
the questions a single simulation answers.

### Process corners

```bash
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli corners ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/design.yaml
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli sensitivity ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/design.yaml   # which parameter moves which metric
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli search ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/design.yaml        # find a configuration meeting the targets
```

Each parameter named in `corners.parameters` is displaced by the excursion given,
one at a time (`onefactor`, 2n+1 runs) or in every combination (`factorial`,
3^n). The spread of each target metric is tabulated and the verdict recorded at
every corner, into `runs/corners.json` and `runs/corners.md`.

On the validation baseline, across ±20 nm on the post gap and ±10 nm on both the
film thickness and the etch depth, the mode-hop-free range moves from 1.9 to
10.0 GHz. **The metric that matters most to a swept source varies by a factor of
five across an ordinary process window**, while the Bragg wavelength moves by
0.9 per cent. A single nominal run cannot show that.

### A foundry rule deck

```yaml
drc:
  deck: smoke_deck.drc        # or a foundry .lydrc
  klayout_exe: null           # found automatically where installed
```

The declared `rules` are evaluated in process by the <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>KLayout</strong></span> Region engine and
are a smoke test. A deck is executed by the <span style="color:#1a73e8"><strong>KLayout</strong></span> application in batch against
the emitted GDS, and its report database is read back and counted by category.
Both run, and both are reported. [`smoke_deck.drc`](../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/smoke_deck.drc)
is a demonstration runset, not a foundry deck; a foundry deck drops into the
same field and needs no change to the chain.

The mechanism was verified against a mask known to violate: on the geometry with
the post gap opened to 762 nm, the deck reports 800 violations of
`METAL_to_WG_sep`, which is exactly what the independent in-process engine
reports.

One field of every rule deserves attention. `ignore_angle_deg` is the interior
angle at or above which a corner is not reported as a width or a space
violation, and it defaults to the engine's 90°. Every convex corner brings two
edges arbitrarily close together, so a check without such a threshold reports
the corner itself. The default is therefore correct only for an orthogonal
layout: an 8° facet on a guide produces an 82° corner at the taper tip, which is
manufacturable and which a minimum-width rule reports at the default setting.

### A layout regression

```bash
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli golden ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/design.yaml
./.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m picchain.cli golden ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline/design.yaml --accept
```

Every solver here is anchored to a closed-form result. The layout stage has no
such anchor, there being no analytic expression for a floor plan, so the only
statement available about it is that it has not changed since it was last
examined. The reference is held in `golden/` beside the design file and is
version controlled. A difference is reported as the exclusive-or area per layer,
which localises the change rather than merely detecting it. Adopting a new
reference is an explicit act, so a change is reviewed once and then stops being
reported.

### A submission manifest

```yaml
release:
  enabled: true
  revision: A
  strict: true                # raise rather than warn
  waive: []                   # conditions deliberately accepted as unmet
```

Producing a manifest asserts that a set of files is ready to be sent. Ten
conditions of readiness are evaluated rather than assumed, and the stage raises
where one is unmet. Each may be waived by name, and the waiver is recorded in
the manifest rather than removing the row.

The manifest inventories every artifact with its SHA-256, the design file and
its hash, the resolved design after every override, and the environment
fingerprint. A recipient holding two masks can then order them, and a sender
holding three can say which was sent.

On the validation baseline, taken to a complete mask with the die frame and the
fill placed, the release is blocked on two conditions: no foundry rule deck has
been executed, and the acceptance targets are not met. Both are true, and both
are outstanding for reasons recorded elsewhere in this repository.

## Correspondence Between Controls and Quantities

Internalisation of the following is required before parameter changes are
attempted.

| quantity to be changed | control | mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Bragg wavelength | `grating.period_um`, or set `target_wavelength_um` and allow the chain to solve the period | λ_B = 2·n̄·Λ/m |
| κ (and hence R and bandwidth) | `grating.post_gap_um`, **first** | κ is *exponentially* sensitive to the gap — a decay rate of approximately 5.4 µm⁻¹ on the TFLN validation baseline, so 20 nm of lithographic error constitutes 11 % of κ |
| κ, secondary control | `grating.post_width_um`, `platform.sidewall_deg` | sloped sidewalls *increase* κ, a wider post base being positioned closer to the ridge |
| peak reflectivity at fixed κ | `grating.length_um` | R = tanh²(κL) |
| mirror bandwidth | `grating.length_um` | the floor is 0.886·c/(2·n_g·L); see `grating.transform_limit_fwhm_GHz` |
| sidelobes | `grating.apodisation` | uniform gratings exhibit first sidelobes at approximately −9 dB, by which the laser mode is pulled and which appear as chirp nonlinearity |
| tuning MHz/V | `electrodes.gap_um` | Δn ∝ 1/G, but Γ falls as G is reduced and metal loss rises; inspect `eo.mode_overlap_with_metal` |
| the mask against the manufacturing grid | `process.grid_nm` | every vertex is snapped after the layer is merged, a boolean introducing vertices at intersections that the drawing never placed. A period that is not an integer number of grid steps acquires a dither, reported as `layout.grid.period_dither_nm_rms`, which is a weak chirp of the grating and is carried by no model here |
| what the process prints against what is drawn | `process.bias_um` per layer, and `process.precompensate` | a width gains the bias and a **gap loses it**, both facing edges advancing into it. With pre-compensation the mask is drawn inward by half the bias so the printed feature lands on the nominal dimension |
| κ under a lithographic bias, on a grating of order m > 1 | as above | two opposing terms: Δn_eff rises as the gap closes, and the harmonic amplitude sin(mπD) falls as the duty cycle moves away from 1/(2m). On the validation baseline a 40 nm bias raised Δn_eff by 24 % and lowered κ by 8 % |
| **mode-hop-free range** | `cavity.rsoa.length_um` ↓, `cavity.feed_length_um` ↓, `grating.post_gap_um` ↑, `grating.length_um` ↑ | MHF ≈ r/(1−r) · FSR/2, where r = `cavity.pockels_lever` = τ_DBR/τ_rt. Weakening the mirror raises r, the penetration depth growing toward L/2, so κ is a control on the tuning range and not only on the reflectivity. It is bounded: at L/2 the range asymptotes, and on the validation baseline that asymptote is 7.47 GHz against an 8 GHz target, so weakening alone cannot reach it. The range is measured between mode hops and not from zero bias; `mode_hop_free_range_from_zero_bias_GHz` is the figure obtained without a DC offset and `bias_offset_needed` states when they differ |
| threshold current and output power | `cavity.rsoa.gain.*`, `dynamics.operating_current_mA` | the rate equations. Every carrier above threshold makes one photon, and `dynamics.output_coupling_fraction` of them leave |
| **whether the laser is stable at all** | `cavity.rsoa.front_facet_R` | an external-cavity laser is operated under strong feedback deliberately, and is stable only while the external mirror dominates the chip facet. `dynamics.external_over_facet_margin_dB` is that margin, and `dynamics.feedback_regime` is the regime. On the validation baseline the margin is 36.8 dB and the regime is V; a facet degrading from 10⁻⁴ to 5 × 10⁻³ takes it to 19.9 dB and regime IV, coherence collapse |
| laser tuning MHz/V | as above | the laser tunes at r × the mirror MHz/V figure, and not at the mirror rate |
| linewidth | `cavity.rsoa.output_power_mW` ↑, mirror R ↑, `cavity.rsoa.length_um` (through the active fraction) | Schawlow–Townes–Henry with the extended-cavity (τ_a/τ_rt)² factor |

**The most consequential derived quantity in this chain is
`cavity.pockels_lever`.** Only the fraction of the round-trip delay residing
within the electro-optically tuned grating follows the mirror when voltage is
applied. A lever of 0.39 (the TFLN validation baseline) indicates that the laser tunes at 39 %
of the mirror efficiency and hops after approximately 2.8 GHz. It is raised
toward unity by shortening everything that is not the grating.


## Sensitivity, and Why It Is Two Tables

`picchain sensitivity` perturbs each declared parameter either side of nominal
and reports two matrices. They answer different questions and neither substitutes
for the other.

**Elasticity**, d(ln metric)/d(ln parameter), is a property of the physics. It
ranks the knobs and does not depend on how well anything is controlled.

**Contribution**, the half-span over the excursion declared in
`corners.parameters`, is a property of the process. It ranks the risks.

The distinction is not academic. On the validation baseline
`eo.mode_overlap_with_metal` carries the largest elasticity in the matrix at
−14.4 against the etch depth, and a 72 % contribution, and is no risk whatever:
it sits three orders of magnitude below its bound. A quantity is at risk only
where the contribution is large **relative to the margin the target leaves**, and
that comparison needs the target as well as both tables.

Two further cautions.

**An elasticity presumes the metric is monotone in the parameter.** Where it is
not, the number depends on the width of the perturbation and means nothing. On
the validation baseline the mode-hop-free range gave +17.2 against the sidewall
angle over a ±2° excursion and +0.9 over a ±5 % probe, a factor of nineteen,
because the metric doubles back within the range. `search` flags the same
condition in its reachability phase. Read the elasticity of a non-monotone metric
as a warning that no derivative describes it.

**A corner summary reports spreads and verdicts, not which target failed where.**
The per-corner failures are the informative part: on the validation baseline the
single-mode condition, a `must` target, fails at three of eight excursions, which
no aggregate spread reveals.


## Searching for a Configuration That Meets the Targets

`picchain search` moves the parameters declared under `search.parameters`. It is
not an optimiser, and its value is in the order of its phases rather than in the
solve.

1. **The problem.** Which targets are unmet and by how much, relative to their
   own bounds. A target whose metric the chosen stage subset does not produce is
   named and set aside, since a search cannot chase a quantity it never computes.
2. **Sensitivity.** Each parameter is probed once and the elasticity
   d(ln metric)/d(ln parameter) is reported against every target metric. One run
   per parameter, and the result is a matrix: it states which knob moves which
   target, with sign and magnitude.
3. **Reachability, before any search.** Each parameter is evaluated at both
   bounds. A requirement outside what a parameter reaches is reported as
   unreachable with the binding bound named, rather than approached until the
   budget is spent. Monotonicity across the bracket is checked in the same pass,
   a metric that is not monotone being unsearchable by bisection.
4. **Solve, then verify everything.** Bisection on the dominant control, then
   every target evaluated at the candidate so that what worsened is reported
   beside what improved.

Two guards are declared rather than inferred. `search.constraints` are
comparisons between dotted fields, evaluated before a point is run; a violating
candidate is skipped rather than clamped, a clamp producing geometry that no
longer matches the model. A constraint that cannot be parsed counts as violated,
so a typo cannot silently disable a check.

Each phase exists because of a specific failure. Reachability exists because the
mode-hop-free range asymptotes at 7.47 GHz as the mirror is weakened, against an
8 GHz target, and no gradient descent discovers that. The monotonicity check
exists because the same range measured from zero bias moved between 0.37 and
6.86 GHz on cavities differing by a fraction of a wavelength. The
verify-everything phase exists because shortening the gain chip widened the range
and doubled the linewidth.


## The Flow Is Comprehensive By Default

**A design declares every stage the chain offers, and a stage omitted from
`stages:` is a question nobody asked rather than a question answered
favourably.** This is a rule and not a preference.

The reason is that a verdict cannot report on evidence that does not exist. The
`verify` stage evaluates the targets it was given, a target may only name a
metric that some stage produced, and a stage that never ran produces nothing.
A design whose `taper`, `fem`, `fdtd`, `bend` and `facet` stages are absent
therefore returns PASS on every target while none of the chain's five
cross-checks has been performed, and nothing in that verdict discloses it.

That is not hypothetical. The validation baseline carried twelve of the
seventeen stages for the whole of its development. Each of the five absentees
had been run once as a one-off probe, and every one of those probes predated the
solved grating period, the changed ridge and etch, the real gain chip, the routed
facet and the enlarged die. The verdict read twelve of twelve throughout.

Three requirements follow.

**Enable the cross-checks even where they cost more than the rest of the chain.**
One mode solve per taper slice costs about three minutes against fifty seconds
for everything else. Cost is a reason to run the chain less often. It is not a
reason to leave a cross-check unperformed.

**Where a stage must be omitted, remove it from `stages:` with a comment stating
why.** An omission on the record is a decision. An omission by default is an
oversight that looks identical to a pass.

**Order is not the author's problem.** `stages:` is closed over its dependencies
and sorted topologically before execution, so the list may be written in any
order. It was previously executed in registration order, which is not the same
thing, and a run naming both `fdtd` and `grating` executed `fdtd` first and
raised.

`picchain dashboard` reports the coverage beside the verdict, and names the
stages that produced nothing. A design report is to state the coverage wherever
it states the verdict.


## Running the Chain Efficiently

The chain is cheap except where it is not, and the difference is three orders of
magnitude. Measured on the validation baseline: `grating` under a second,
`facet` 12 s, `fem` 18 s, `mode` 33 s, `bend` 40 s, `taper` 123 s, and the
external band structure **3.2 hours**. One stage is 98 % of a full run.

**Work in three tiers, and choose deliberately between them.**

| tier | stages | cost | when |
|---|---|---|---|
| iterate | `mode,grating,eo,cavity` | about 1 min | changing a parameter and reading its effect |
| decide | everything except `fdtd` | about 15 min | before accepting a change, and at every decision point |
| confirm | the whole list | hours | once per frozen configuration |

**A sweep multiplies whatever it is given, so what is correct for one run is not
correct for nine.** `corners` derives its stages from the metrics it declares
rather than inheriting the design's list. Making the flow comprehensive turned a
nine-corner sweep into a thirty-one hour job until that was fixed, and the
symptom was invisible because nothing reported per-stage cost.

**An identical external solve is never repeated.** The job is content-hashed with
floats rounded to ten significant figures, and a matching prior result is reused
and attributed. Two runs of the same geometry differ in the fifteenth
significant figure through evaluation order alone, so a raw hash never matches:
that is how a three-hour band structure came to be re-queued for a structure
that had not changed. Set `PICCHAIN_NO_REUSE` to force a fresh solve.

**Ask what a run can possibly tell you before starting it.** The re-queued band
structure could not have produced a different answer, `grating.profile_sigma_um`
not entering the solver's job at all. The check that mattered was arithmetic and
took a second. A run that cannot change a conclusion is not evidence, it is
delay.

**Time is recorded on two clocks and both are needed.** Wall clock counts machine
suspend; processor time does not. A corner left overnight reported 8.2 hours
against 70 seconds for its neighbours, and a defect was reported against a period
solver that did not have one. Where a stage delegates to an external process,
that process's own reported time is the one to believe.

**Slow is not stuck, and unfinished is not failed.** Every wrong call about this
chain's health has had the same shape: a 20-minute solve killed as a hang, a
3-hour run declared dead at 13 minutes, an 11-minute sweep killed twice by a
9.8-minute timeout. Before concluding that a job has failed, establish what it
should cost and how far in it is. `picchain dashboard` reports a stage now
running with its elapsed time beside the longest it has previously taken, for
exactly this purpose.


## The Dashboard

`picchain dashboard` writes one self-contained HTML file per run, openable from
disk. It is produced alongside `report.md` whenever `picchain report` is run.

It exists to draw a distinction a verdict cannot. A run reports PASS when every
target it was given is met, and a target may only name a metric that some stage
produced. A design whose `taper`, `fem`, `fdtd`, `bend` and `facet` stages are
switched off therefore passes without any of them having been performed, and the
page states the stage coverage beside the verdict for that reason.

Three things on it are not in the metric tree.

**When each stage last produced a result, anywhere under `runs/`.** A subset run
leaves the other stages untouched, so their absence from one metric tree means
they were not asked rather than that they failed. Each card names the most recent
run that produced that stage.

**Whether the design has changed since.** The resolved design of that earlier run
is compared against the current one. The comparison is over the whole file, so it
is conservative: it may call a result stale that remains valid, and it cannot
call a stale result current. On the validation baseline every one of the five
cross-checks last ran against a different configuration.

**Headroom against each target.** The distance from the value to the bound that
constrains it, as a fraction of that bound, so a quantity three orders inside its
limit is told apart from one 2 % inside it. It is not margin against the process,
which is what `picchain corners` answers.

The warnings are grouped by what must be done about them. The grouping is a
heuristic over the warning text, and anything it does not recognise stays in the
least severe group, so a warning is only ever demoted by it.


## The Run Register

**Every design report carries a table of what was run, when, with which
parameters, and what came of it.** This is a rule and not a convention. A report
states conclusions; the register states the evidence those conclusions rest on,
and without it a reader cannot tell a figure obtained from a converged
twenty-five point map from one obtained by a single probe.

Each row records:

| column | what it must say |
|---|---|
| date | when the study was executed |
| study | the command, and what question it was asked |
| parameters | the fields varied and the values they took |
| runs | how many evaluations the study cost |
| outcome | what it established, including where it established nothing |

Three requirements follow from experience rather than from tidiness.

**A study that was superseded stays in the register, marked as superseded.** The
band-structure cross-check was run four times before it converged, and the first
three produced a verdict that was withdrawn. Deleting them would leave a reader
unable to see that the surviving number is the fourth attempt.

**A study whose result was discarded says so and says why.** A film-etch probe
was run at the wrong nominal film thickness and its four rows were meaningless.
That is worth a line, since the alternative is a reader finding those runs under
`runs/` and believing them.

**The parameters recorded are the ones actually resolved, not the ones
intended.** Every run directory stores `design.resolved.json` for this reason.


## The Concept of Operation, and Why the Targets Are Traced to It

Every design carries a `DESIGN_CONCEPT.md` beside its `design.yaml`. It states
what the device does, by what principle it does it, and which physical quantity
expresses each clause of that principle. **The acceptance targets are derived
from those clauses**, and the concept ends with a trace table binding each clause
to the target that tests it:

```
| clause | target metric | severity |
|---|---|---|
| the sweep is continuous | `cavity.mode_hop_free_range_synchronous_GHz` | must |
| a lost phase drive degrades | `cavity.mode_hop_free_range_placed_GHz` | should |
```

`tools/check_concept_trace.py` compares that table against the declared targets
and reports three kinds of discrepancy:

```bash
$PY tools/check_concept_trace.py ../examples/edbr_tfln_baseline
$PY tools/check_concept_trace.py ../examples/*/          # several at once
```

* **a target declared and absent from the concept** — the design is graded on
  something the concept does not ask for, which is where an inherited target
  hides;
* **a clause traced to a metric no target declares** — the concept promises what
  the validation does not test;
* **a severity disagreement** between the two.

Exit code 0 means every target traces, 1 a discrepancy, 2 a missing file.

### The failure this exists to prevent

**A target carries a mechanism as well as a number.** Where a design replaces
the mechanism, the target stops testing the requirement and starts testing the
absence of the change. There is no symptom: the run completes, the metric is
computed, the corner sweep returns a spread.

A laser was built with a second electrode holding its mode comb in step with its
mirror, so that no mode hop occurs. Its targets were inherited from a laser built
on the opposite concept of positioning the hop outside the sweep, so the `must`
row measured a comb left to slip. The new device read 8.41 GHz against the older
design's 11.22, and 17 of 81 process corners failed. Re-derived from its own
concept it gives 12.11 GHz continuously against 5.61 guaranteed, and every corner
clears. **The verdict inverted on which concept the target encoded**, and not on
any property of the device.

Rereading the documents does not find this. Each was internally consistent, and
only comparing the targets against a written statement of the principle exposes
it. That is why the trace is a table checked by a tool rather than a paragraph
of prose.

### Two obligations

* **When the architecture changes, re-derive the targets from the new concept**
  rather than inheriting them. Where the old row still describes a real degraded
  mode, keep it at a lower severity so the degradation stays on the record.
* **Validate against the concept**, so that a passing run is evidence about this
  device rather than about the one before it.

## Addition of a Stage

1. Write `src/picchain/stages/sNN_<name>.py`, exposing
   `run(design, ctx, lib) -> dict`.
2. Read upstream results by means of `ctx.get("grating")`, and upstream arrays
   from `ctx.run_dir / "grating.npz"`.
3. Write results by means of `ctx.put("<name>", payload)` and
   `ctx.write_stage("<name>", payload, arrays)`.
4. Register the stage in `stages/__init__.py` (`STAGES` and `DEPENDENCIES`).
5. Add a closed-form test to `tests/`. Solvers are validated against analytic
   results and not against recorded outputs; a regression test that pins only the
   previous numerical result will equally pin the previous defect.

## Design Philosophy

**The physics core is held free of heavy dependencies.** The FD mode solver and
the electrostatic solver comprise approximately 200 lines each of numpy/scipy.
The trade is deliberate: a chain requiring a working `gmsh`, `meep` or `jax`
installation on every machine is a chain that cannot be executed headlessly under
continuous integration, and the accuracy of consequence here resides in
*differences* evaluated on a shared mesh (Δn_eff ≈ 8e-4 between cross-sections
with and without Bragg posts), where systematic discretisation error is largely
cancelled. Heavier solvers are appropriately positioned within the chain as
*optional cross-checks* rather than on the critical path.

**Every acceptance criterion is held with the design rather than in the code.** A
design is regarded as complete only against a declared and sourced target list. A
non-zero exit code is returned by `verify` where a `must` target is unmet, and it
is by this property that the chain is rendered automatable.

**Provenance is recorded as a schema field.** A `provenance` string is carried by
each material; `needs_confirmation` entries are reported on every run and may be
configured as a hard block. An `# ASSUMPTION:` comment is carried by each design
file quantity that cannot be traced to the literature, to a project statement
of requirements, or to a foundry document.
